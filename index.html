From farmland to token

How a regulated real-world asset becomes a compliant token on Redbelly Network, through Tokeniser. One Australian farmland fund, four steps, 87 seconds, in plain language.

1:26 runtime 1080p 60 fps Subtitles on 23 claims, each sourced

The journey

The asset stays the same. The record of who owns it becomes a token.

The fund keeps its farmland. What changes is how ownership of its units is recorded and moved: as tokens with the fund's rules programmed in.

Step 1

Set up

The fund is verified and its rules, such as wholesale investors only, are encoded into the token.

Step 2

Investors

Each investor is verified through Averer, then gets a wallet on Redbelly Network to hold tokens.

Step 3

Issue

Tokens are minted on Redbelly Network into verified wallets, and the register updates immediately.

Step 4

Transfer

Every transfer is checked against the rules first. A compliant one settles on Redbelly in seconds and is final once confirmed.

The platform the manager uses

A familiar platform for issuing units as tokens, onboarding investors and running the register.

The network underneath

Redbelly Network, Australia's public digital token infrastructure, where tokens are minted, held in wallets and settled.

How it was made

One source of truth, checked on every build

The script file drives the voice, the subtitles, the animation, the documents and this page, so none of them can drift apart.

  1. Script with sources. content/script.json holds every line, every on-screen word and the quote behind every claim.
  2. Voice. Each line is spoken with Piper (Alba voice), several takes per line, and speech recognition keeps the clearest.
  3. Timing. Word timestamps from the voice drive every cue in the animation and the subtitles.
  4. Animation. An HTML canvas scene where every frame is a pure function of time, rendered frame by frame at 60 fps.
  5. Sound. Music and effects are synthesised from code, ducked under the voice and mixed to -16 LUFS.
  6. Checks. Quotes, subtitles, on-screen text, wording rules, video specs and loudness are verified automatically.
